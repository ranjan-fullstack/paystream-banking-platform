RBI GUIDELINES: REAL TIME GROSS SETTLEMENT (RTGS)

1. OPERATING HOURS
RTGS customer transactions are processed on all days of the week (RBI moved
RTGS to 24x7x365 availability effective December 14, 2020, per Circular
RBI/2020-21/70). However, many bank-facing implementations, including this
platform, continue to model RTGS around its historical settlement window for
straight-through-processing purposes:
  - Monday to Friday: 07:00 AM to 5:45 PM
  - Saturday: 07:00 AM to 1:45 PM
Transactions submitted outside these windows should be queued and settled at
the next available window, or rejected with an appropriate "outside
processing hours" reason code, depending on the bank's operating model.

2. TRANSACTION LIMITS
- Minimum amount: Rs 2,00,000 (Rs 2 lakh) — this is an RBI-mandated floor;
  RTGS exists specifically for large-value, time-critical transactions.
- Maximum amount: No RBI-prescribed ceiling. Individual banks may impose
  their own operational maximum based on risk appetite and channel (branch
  vs. net/mobile banking).
- Transactions below Rs 2,00,000 must be rejected and the customer directed
  to use NEFT, IMPS, or UPI instead.

3. REAL-TIME GROSS SETTLEMENT
Unlike NEFT (deferred net settlement in batches), RTGS settles each
transaction individually and immediately upon processing — "gross" meaning
transaction-by-transaction (not netted) and "real-time" meaning as soon as
processed, without waiting for a batch window. Once settled at the RBI RTGS
system, the transaction is final and irrevocable.

4. UTR NUMBER FORMAT
Every RTGS transaction is assigned a Unique Transaction Reference (UTR)
number, exactly 22 characters long, typically structured as:
  <IFSC first 4 chars><Julian date/year><sequence padded to make 22 chars>
e.g., HDFCR52025010200001234. This UTR is the authoritative reference for
tracing a transaction across the sending bank, RBI's RTGS system, and the
receiving bank.

5. RTGS PURPOSE CODES
RTGS remittances may carry an optional purpose code (used mainly for
corporate/high-value payments) indicating the nature of the transaction,
e.g., "SALARY", "SUPPLIER_PAYMENT", "LOAN_DISBURSEMENT", "TAX_PAYMENT",
"SECURITIES_SETTLEMENT". Purpose codes assist in AML/CFT monitoring and
regulatory reporting.

6. RTGS REJECTION REASONS
- Amount below the Rs 2,00,000 regulatory minimum
- Submission outside the bank's configured RTGS processing window
- Invalid/incorrect IFSC or beneficiary account number
- Beneficiary account frozen, closed, or KYC-non-compliant
- Insufficient funds/limit in the remitter's account
- Beneficiary bank RTGS-disabled or temporarily unavailable in the RTGS
  network

7. SETTLEMENT FINALITY
RTGS settlement is final and irrevocable once the beneficiary bank's account
is credited at the RBI settlement layer — there is no batch-level netting or
recall mechanism analogous to NEFT returns. Any correction after settlement
must be handled as a fresh reverse transaction agreed between the parties,
not a technical "unwind" of the original RTGS transfer. This makes accurate
beneficiary details critical before submission.
