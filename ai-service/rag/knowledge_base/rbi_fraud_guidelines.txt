RBI GUIDELINES: FRAUD PREVENTION IN DIGITAL PAYMENTS

1. GENERAL FRAUD PREVENTION FRAMEWORK
RBI's Master Directions on Digital Payment Security Controls and the Master
Direction on Fraud Risk Management require regulated entities to implement
real-time and near-real-time transaction monitoring, layered authentication,
and risk-based transaction limits across all digital payment rails
(NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI). Payment processors are expected to combine rule-based
and behavioral/ML-based detection to flag anomalous transactions before or
immediately after settlement.

2. VELOCITY CHECK THRESHOLDS
Velocity checks monitor the rate of transactions from a single account or
device within a rolling time window. Common industry/RBI-aligned thresholds:
- More than 5 transactions within a 10-minute window from the same account
  is considered anomalous and should be flagged for review.
- More than 3 transactions to the same beneficiary within 5 minutes should
  be treated as a potential duplicate-payment or automated-fraud pattern.
- A sudden spike in transaction count relative to the customer's 30-day
  rolling average (e.g., 5x normal daily volume) should raise the risk
  score even if individual transactions appear unremarkable.

3. UNUSUAL TRANSACTION PATTERNS
- Amount anomaly: A transaction more than 3x the customer's historical
  average transaction amount, especially to a new/unregistered beneficiary.
- Odd-hour transfers: High-value NEFT/RTGS transactions initiated late at
  night (e.g., after 11 PM) are statistically over-represented in fraud
  cases and should receive elevated scrutiny, particularly for first-time
  beneficiaries.
- Geo/device anomaly: Transaction originating from a device or IP
  inconsistent with the customer's established pattern.
- New beneficiary + large first-transaction amount: Fraud rings frequently
  add a new mule beneficiary and immediately push a large-value transfer.

4. AML (ANTI MONEY LAUNDERING) RULES
Under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) and RBI's Master
Direction on KYC, regulated entities must:
- Maintain a risk-based customer due diligence (CDD) programme.
- Monitor for transaction patterns consistent with layering, placement, or
  integration stages of money laundering.
- File Cash Transaction Reports (CTR) and Suspicious Transaction Reports
  (STR) with the Financial Intelligence Unit-India (FIU-IND) as required.

5. CTR (CASH TRANSACTION REPORT) THRESHOLD
Cash transactions (individually or as an interconnected series) aggregating
to Rs 10,00,000 or more in a calendar month must be reported via a CTR to
FIU-IND. Note: for electronic/digital payment processors specifically,
platform-level "large value transaction" monitoring commonly references
Rs 50,00,000 (Rs 50 lakh) as an internal escalation threshold for
enhanced due diligence review on non-cash, high-value fund movements,
even though the statutory CTR requirement is cash-specific.

6. STRUCTURING DETECTION
Structuring ("smurfing") is the practice of splitting a large transaction
into multiple smaller transactions to evade reporting or approval
thresholds. Detection heuristics:
- Multiple transactions just below the RTGS/NEFT threshold of interest
  (e.g., three or more transactions of Rs 9,00,000-Rs 9,99,999 within a
  short window, deliberately staying under the Rs 10,00,000 NEFT ceiling
  or an internal Rs 10 lakh review threshold).
- Multiple transactions to the same or related beneficiaries within a
  short time span that, if aggregated, would exceed a single-transaction
  review or reporting threshold.
- Rapid-fire transactions across different payment rails (NEFT + IMPS +
  UPI) to the same beneficiary in a short window.

7. PEP (POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSON) SCREENING
Customers and beneficiaries must be screened against PEP lists (domestic
and foreign) as part of onboarding and periodically thereafter. Transactions
involving a PEP counterparty should receive enhanced due diligence and a
higher baseline risk score, per RBI's KYC Master Direction.

8. KYC REQUIREMENTS BEFORE PAYMENT
No payment instruction should be processed for an account that has not
completed at least minimum/simplified KYC. Full KYC is required before
high-value RTGS transactions or before limits are enhanced. Payments
involving a beneficiary account flagged as "KYC non-compliant" or "under
review" should be blocked pending resolution.

9. SUSPICIOUS TRANSACTION REPORT (STR) REQUIREMENTS
Any transaction, or a series of related transactions, that a regulated
entity has reason to suspect involves proceeds of crime, is designed to
evade reporting requirements, or has no apparent economic/lawful purpose,
must be reported as an STR to FIU-IND, regardless of the transaction
amount. There is no minimum threshold for STR filing, unlike CTR.

10. COMMON FRAUD PATTERNS IN NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI
- Vishing/phishing-induced UPI COLLECT approval, where the victim is
  tricked into approving a COLLECT request believing it to be a receipt.
- Account takeover followed by rapid IMPS/UPI transfers to mule accounts
  before the legitimate customer notices.
- Fake customer-support numbers directing victims to share OTP/UPI PIN.
- Business email compromise leading to fraudulent RTGS/NEFT transfers
  to attacker-controlled accounts disguised as a genuine vendor.
- SIM-swap fraud enabling interception of OTPs used for UPI registration
  or high-value transaction authorization.
